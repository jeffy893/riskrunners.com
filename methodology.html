How it works

Methodology

From 311,000 risk-factor sentences to 30 hedgeable portfolios.

The Pure-Play Thesis

If a private investment and a public company share the same risk factors, the public company is a pure play on that risk. Shorting it hedges the private holding: the event that impairs one impairs the other. Pure Play finds those matches by grouping public companies according to the risks they actually disclose.

The Pipeline

  1. Extract. Every Item 1A "Risk Factors" section from 2,301 companies' 2019 10-K filings — 311,514 individual risk statements — parsed to clean text.
  2. Vectorize & cluster. Each company becomes a TF-IDF document over its risk language; k-means groups companies that describe the same risks into 30 portfolios.
  3. Analyze. Each portfolio is named and framed as an event horizon, with the single ISO 31010 technique that best fits its dominant uncertainty.
  4. Publish. Self-contained report pages with search and cross-links, deployable as a static site.

ISO 31010 Techniques Applied

  • Bow Tie Analysis
  • Cross Impact Analysis
  • Ishikawa (Fishbone)
  • Risk-Theme Prevalence

Each portfolio uses the technique matched to its risk structure: distributional risks get Monte Carlo, binary-outcome risks get Decision Trees, state-transition risks get Markov analysis, and so on.

Why a 2019 Baseline

Post-2020 filings are dominated by pandemic language that nearly every company added at once, which washes out the structural differences between businesses. The 2019 set is a cleaner signal of the risks that genuinely separate one company from another.
