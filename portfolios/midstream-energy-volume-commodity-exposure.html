Midstream Energy Volume & Commodity Exposure

Midstream operators whose cash flows collapse when hydrocarbon production, prices, or transport volumes decline.

48 companiesPure-play hedge portfolio2019 baseline

Event Horizon

A sustained crude oil or natural gas price collapse (or production slowdown in key regions) triggers lower upstream capex, reduced well drilling, and declining reserves—shrinking the volume of hydrocarbons flowing through gathering, processing, and pipeline infrastructure. All 48 companies in this cluster depend on throughput; a shared supply-side shock moves them together. An investor holding private midstream assets (e.g., a gathering system or processing plant) would short this public portfolio to hedge volume risk.

Shared Risk Factors

  • Commodity price sensitivity: fees tied to natural gas, NGL, or crude oil price indices; margin compression when prices fall
  • Throughput volume dependency: profitability directly tied to volumes of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil transported, gathered, processed, or fractionated
  • Reserve depletion: existing wells naturally decline; new exploration and development capital-intensive and cyclical, especially offshore Gulf of Mexico
  • Upstream capex linkage: depressed commodity prices reduce exploration and drilling activity, lowering future feedstock to midstream facilities
  • Regional production concentration: facilities dependent on hydrocarbons from specific geographic basins; basin-level production shocks cascade to throughput
  • Regulatory and FERC exposure: pipeline tariffs, rate-setting, and environmental permitting create operational and margin uncertainty
  • Seasonal and demand volatility: natural gas and NGL demand fluctuates seasonally and with economic cycles, creating revenue instability

Monte Carlo Simulation

This cluster's dominant uncertainty is the joint distribution of commodity prices, upstream production volumes, and exploration capex—all continuous, correlated variables with fat-tailed downside risk. Monte Carlo allows modeling of price-volume correlation, reserve depletion curves, and capex sensitivity simultaneously, quantifying portfolio-level value-at-risk and optimal hedge ratios across the 48 companies.

The Pure-Play Hedge

An investor with private exposure to midstream infrastructure (gathering, processing, fractionation, or pipeline assets) faces volume and commodity-price risk. Shorting this 48-company public portfolio hedges that exposure: when upstream production declines or commodity prices collapse, both the private asset and the public companies suffer lower throughput and margins simultaneously. The hedge is pure-play because all companies share identical volume and commodity sensitivities.

Representative Risk-Factor Language

  • We operate predominantly in the midstream energy sector which includes gathering, transporting, processing, fractionating and storing natural gas, NGLs and crude oil
  • Generally, the prices of natural gas, NGLs, crude oil and other hydrocarbon products are subject to fluctuations in response to changes in supply, demand, market uncertainty and a variety of additional factors that are impossible to control
  • These factors include: § the level of domestic production; § the availability of imported oil and natural gas; § actions taken by foreign oil and natural gas producing nations; § the availability of transportation systems with adequate capacity; § the availability of competitive fuels; § fluctuating and seasonal demand for oil, natural gas and NGLs; and § conservation and the extent of governmental regulation of production and the overall economic environment
  • We are exposed to natural gas and NGL commodity price risk under certain of our natural gas processing and gathering and NGL fractionation contracts that provide for our fees to be calculated based on a regional natural gas or NGL price index or to be paid in-kind by taking title to natural gas or NGLs
  • A decrease in natural gas and NGL prices can result in lower margins from these contracts, which may materially adversely affect our results of operations, cash flows and financial position
  • A decline in the volume of natural gas, NGLs and crude oil delivered to our facilities could adversely affect our results of operations, cash flows and financial condition
  • Our profitability could be materially impacted by a decline in the volume of natural gas, NGLs and crude oil transported, gathered or processed at our facilities
  • A material decrease in natural gas or crude oil production or crude oil refining, as a result of depressed commodity prices, a decrease in exploration and development activities or otherwise, could result in a decline in the volume of natural gas, NGLs and crude oil handled by our facilities
  • The crude oil, natural gas and NGLs available to our facilities will be derived from reserves produced from existing wells, which reserves naturally decline over time
  • To offset this natural decline, our facilities will need access to additional reserves
