Raw Material Cost Pass-Through Cyclicals

Manufacturers dependent on commodity input costs face margin compression when unable to pass price increases to price-sensitive, cyclical customers.

167 companiesPure-play hedge portfolio2019 baseline

Event Horizon

A sustained spike in crude oil, metals, or chemical feedstock prices—coupled with weak customer demand in cyclical end-markets (automotive, construction, electronics)—creates a scissor: input costs rise while selling prices stagnate. These 167 companies all face this dual squeeze simultaneously because they share the same supplier base, customer verticals, and limited pricing power. A private equity stake in a specialty-chemical or industrial-products manufacturer would suffer identical margin erosion; shorting this public cluster hedges that exposure.

Shared Risk Factors

  • Inability to pass through raw material and energy cost increases to customers on a timely basis, eroding product margins
  • Dependence on cyclical end-markets (automotive, construction, electronics) whose downturns reduce demand and pricing power simultaneously
  • Supply-chain constraints: raw material suppliers may extend lead times or limit availability, disrupting production and sales
  • Intense competition from larger, better-capitalized rivals who can absorb commodity-price volatility and undercut pricing
  • Exposure to volatile commodity prices (crude oil, metals, chlorine) with limited hedging or long-term fixed-price contracts
  • Manufacturing and production complexity requiring high-volume efficiency to offset margin compression from price competition
  • International and currency exposure in sourcing and customer sales, amplifying input-cost and demand volatility

The Pure-Play Hedge

An investor holding private equity in a specialty-chemical manufacturer, plastics processor, or industrial-products company faces margin risk when commodity inputs spike and customers (automakers, builders, appliance makers) cut orders. Shorting this 167-company cluster—which includes ALBEMARLE, POLYONE, SCHULMAN, COMMERCIAL METALS, HONEYWELL, and peers—creates a pure-play hedge: the same commodity-price shock and cyclical downturn that crushes private-company EBITDA will depress these public equities' earnings and multiples in lockstep.
