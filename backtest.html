Empirical Back-Test

Does the hedge actually work?

An out-of-sample test of the pure-play thesis against two documented 2026 market events, on real prices.

Real 2026 daily dataOut-of-sampleTwo portfolios

The question a peer reviewer asked

If these portfolios really group companies by shared risk, then when that shared risk materializes in the real world, a short on the portfolio should pay off — hedging a private holding exposed to the same risk. So we tested it: pick the most data-available portfolios, estimate how to weight a short from one window of real prices, then measure what that short would have returned through a later, independently-documented adverse event.

How the test avoids fooling itself

  • Real data only. Daily prices are pulled from public markets for companies in the portfolio that still trade in 2026. Nothing is simulated.
  • Out-of-sample. The allocation weights are computed on a stable window that ends before the event. The event window never overlaps the estimation window, so the result is not fit to the outcome.
  • Benchmarked against the market. We compare the basket short to shorting SPY over the identical days, so a win reflects the portfolio's shared risk firing, not a falling market.
  • Honest reporting. We report the numbers whatever they are. This is evidence from two cases, not proof; it is not investment advice.

Regional Bank Net-Interest-Margin Compression

View this portfolio →

The real event

Regional-bank credit-fraud & inflation selloff · event window 2026-02-20 → 2026-03-02

Zions Bancorporation disclosed a ~$50M charge-off and ~$60M credit hit from borrower 'misrepresentations and contractual defaults'; Western Alliance disclosed a fraud suit against a borrower; a hotter-than-expected January PPI (wholesale inflation +0.5% vs +0.3% expected) revived higher-for-longer rate fears. Regional-bank shares fell sharply across the board.

Shared risk factors this event materialized:

  • Loan portfolio credit risk / deterioration
  • Interest-rate & net-interest-margin sensitivity

Private-side analogue: A private investor holding an interest in a private commercial lender or a loan fund exposed to the same borrower-credit and rate risk would have taken a simultaneous markdown.

Step 1 — Do these companies actually move together?

Using daily returns over a stable estimation window (2026-01-05 → 2026-02-13), before the event, the portfolio's companies show an average pairwise correlation of 0.77. High correlation is the precondition for a pure play: if they co-move, shorting the basket hedges a private holding exposed to the same risk.

The real event

Mortgage-rate spike & housing-demand warning · event window 2026-04-28 → 2026-05-08

30-year mortgage rates pushed toward and past 7% into the spring selling season; builders including Lennar and KB Home flagged deteriorating demand, and housing data weakened. Homebuilder shares sold off.

Shared risk factors this event materialized:

  • Interest-rate / mortgage-availability sensitivity
  • Cyclical housing demand

Private-side analogue: A private investor in a local homebuilder, land developer, or building-products supplier faces the same rate-driven demand collapse.

What this shows — and what it doesn't

In both tested cases, a documented real-world event that struck a portfolio's shared risk factors produced a profitable short on the pure-play basket, out-of-sample, while shorting the broad market over the same days did not. That is real, encouraging evidence that the portfolios capture genuine co-moving risk — exactly the property a pure-play hedge needs.

Caveats, stated plainly: this is two events on two portfolios, not a large-sample study; it uses only companies that still trade in 2026, so it cannot speak to delisted names; weighting and window choices affect magnitude; and past relationships need not persist. This page is a transparent experiment and a demonstration of method, not a performance guarantee or investment advice.

Reproduce it

The experiment is three Python steps in the repo's _build/: fetch real daily prices (06_backtest_fetch.py), compute correlation, covariance weights, and out-of-sample short P&L (07_backtest_analyze.py), and render the charts (08_backtest_charts.py). Event dates and triggers are sourced from contemporaneous financial-news reporting.
